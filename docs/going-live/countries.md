# Country rules

The app ships with 20 markets. Rules are scoped to the selected one — someone
paying in Cambodia never sees India's tax regime, which is noise at best and
misleading at worst.

## The distinction that matters

**Legal to hold ≠ legal to use as payment.** A jurisdiction can permit owning
and trading an asset while its central bank opposes it as a payment instrument.
That single distinction is often the entire blocker, and it is easy to miss when
reading headlines about whether crypto is "legal" somewhere.

## Best fit

**Cambodia.** Heavily dollarised, so USDC is close to what people already use.
The January 2025 NBC framework recognises fully-backed stablecoins as a
permitted asset class. KHQR is one national standard covering every bank and
wallet. No withholding tax eating your margin.

**Singapore** for the entity — a clear, well-tested stablecoin framework.

## Hardest, despite being the biggest

**India.** Holding and trading is legal, and dozens of platforms hold FIU
registration. But using crypto *as a payment instrument* is the specific thing
the central bank has said should not happen, and the one-tap flow requires
pooling rupees — which triggers payment-aggregator licensing from that same
regulator.

Add a 1% withholding on every transfer and mandatory PAN/Aadhaar verification
before any rupee moves, and you have a market that demands two years of
licensing before the first transaction, into a regulator leaning restrictive.

Worth entering eventually. Not worth entering first.

## Not addressable

**China.** The February 2026 circular reaffirms that all virtual-currency
business is illegal financial activity, extends the ban to stablecoins, and
explicitly prohibits overseas entities from serving people inside the country.
Being incorporated elsewhere does not create a route in. The app lists it as
blocked with the reason surfaced.

## Tourist-only

**Thailand.** Paying merchants in crypto is banned. The only legal route is the
TouristDigiPay sandbox: foreign visitors convert through a licensed operator and
the merchant is paid in baht. You cannot serve Thai residents.

## Africa runs on mobile money

Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana lead the world in stablecoin transaction use, but
settlement is **M-Pesa, MTN MoMo and Airtel** — not QR codes. Same wallet,
genuinely different plumbing. Budget for it as a separate integration rather
than a config change.

## Sequencing

Prove the product where it is legal and the economics work. Enter the hard
market later with revenue and a working business rather than a deck. If the
rules there loosen you are ready; if they tighten, you did not bet the company
on it.
